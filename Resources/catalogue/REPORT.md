## Catalogue corrections

- `gtag`, `googleads` (Universal Analytics names): `__utma`, `__utmb`, `__utmc`, `__utmt`, `__utmz` (`googleads`) and `_gid`, `_gat`, `_gat_gtag_*` (both) are removed from their lists. They belong to Universal Analytics, which GA4 does not set: after consent a browser holds only `_ga` and `_ga_<id>` (measured 2026-11-01, `gtag`). The correction lives in `tools/catalogue/verified.json`, so a new import keeps it.
- `gtag`: `_gcl_au` is removed. It is the advertising cookie of the conversion linker (https://business.safety.google/adscookies/), never set by GA4 on 3 passes after "Accept all" (2026-11-01): `ad_storage` stays `denied` while no advertising purpose is active. It stays on `googleads` and `googletagmanager`. Not measured: `_gcl_au` under an active advertising purpose.
- `youtube`: `__Secure-YNID`, `__Secure-ROLLOUT_TOKEN` (documented by Google) and `VISITOR_PRIVACY_METADATA` (measured only, on `www.youtube.com`, 3 passes, absent on `youtube-nocookie`) are added to `VISITOR_INFO1_LIVE`, `YSC`, `PREF` (documented, `PREF` not set in the measure). `storage` lists the 3 keys of the player written in localStorage, on `youtube-nocookie` too: `ytidb::LAST_RESULT_ENTRY_KEY`, `yt-player-caption-persistence`, `yt-icons-last-purged`. Under a preset tied to the main language, `VISITOR_PRIVACY_METADATA` without a duration blocks the publication until the administrator types one.
- `googletagmanager`: Tag Manager sets no cookie by itself, those of a container depend on the tags the publisher puts in it. The list is cut to the cookies that have an official source and a duration (`_ga`, `_ga_*`, `_gcl_au`); the legacy `__utm*`, `_gat` and the third-party advertising cookies (`__gads`, `_drt_`, `FLC`, `id`, `uid`...) have no source and are removed. The publisher declares the cookies of its other tags as a custom service.
- `analytics` (Universal Analytics) is excluded (reason `universal-analytics`, table `EXCLUDED_BY_KEY` in `tools/catalogue/purposes.mjs`): Google shut the product down, and its fragment hands `timeExpire` (milliseconds) to `cookieExpires` (`fragments/analytics.js` line 31 in v1.37.0), which expects seconds, whereas the `gtag` fragment divides by 1000. A draft that still enables it is refused with a message that points to `gtag`.
